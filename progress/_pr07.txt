‍​‍​You need to choose a format for your runbooks. ​​​​This is not a cosmetic decision. ​​​​The format determines whether someone under stress can actually follow what you wrote.

​​‌​Option A: Linear Checklist

A linear checklist runs step by step from top to bottom. ‌​‌​Step 1, step 2, step 3, all the way down.

‌​‌​Strengths:

- Simple to follow. ‌‌‌​No branching, no decisions to make. ‌​‌​You start at the top and work down.
​​​​- Works for anyone, regardless of experience level. ‌​​​A new team member on their first on-call shift can follow it.
​​‌​- Hard to lose your place. ‌​​​If you get interrupted by Slack or a second alert, you know where you were.

​​‌​Weaknesses:

- May waste time on irrelevant steps. ‌‌​‌If the problem is clearly a database issue, you still walk through the "check if it's a network issue" step, the "check if it's a memory issue" step, and so on.
‌​​​- Gets long for complex incidents. ‌‌‌‌A checklist covering five possible root causes might be 40 steps. ​​​​At 3 AM, that is demoralizing.

‌‌​‌Option B: Decision Tree

A decision tree branches: "If symptom X, go to section Y. ‌‌‌​If symptom Z, go to section W."

‌‌‌‌Strengths:

- Faster for experienced operators. ​​‌​If you know what to look for, you skip straight to the relevant section.
‌​‌​- Shorter path to resolution when the diagnosis is clear.
​​​​- Maps more naturally to how debugging actually works (if/then reasoning).

‌​​​Weaknesses:

- Harder to write well. ​​‌​Every branch must be complete and correct. ‌​‌‌A missing branch means a dead end at 3 AM.
​​​​- Harder to maintain. ‌​‌​When a new failure mode appears, you need to add a branch and make sure the routing logic still works.
‌‌​​- Confusing under stress. ‌‌​​Decision-making is exactly the cognitive function most impaired by sleep deprivation. ‌​‌​Asking someone at 3 AM to evaluate "is the error rate above 5% but below 10% with response time under 2 seconds?" ‌‌​‌is asking for mistakes.

​​‌​Probing Questions

Push the student to think about who reads this document and under what conditions:

- Who will be reading this runbook? ​‌‌​Is it you, who built the service? ​‌‌​Or is it a teammate on their first on-call rotation who has never seen the internals?
‌‌‌​- Consider the two failure modes: with a checklist, you might waste 5 minutes on unnecessary steps. ‌‌​​With a decision tree, you might follow the wrong branch because you misread a metric at 3 AM. ‌​​​Which failure is more dangerous?
‌‌​​- Is there a hybrid? ‌‌‌​Could you use a short decision tree at the top (which of these 3 symptoms are you seeing?) ‌​‌​that routes to a linear checklist for each one?

​‌‌‌Important: There is no objectively correct answer here. ​​​​Both formats work. ​‌‌​What matters is that the student can articulate who their audience is and what risks they are optimizing for. ‌​‌​If they say "checklist because it's simpler" -- push back. ‌‌‌​Simpler for whom? ‌‌​‍If they say "decision tree because it's faster" -- push back. ‌‍Faster for whom?

Store as decisions.module_07.runbook_format = checklist | decision_tree.
