Interlude: GitLab Deletes Their Production Database

Delivered between Module 6 (Caching) and Module 7 (Background Jobs)

---

January 31, 2017. ‍​‍​​23:00 UTC.

​​​​​A GitLab engineer is troubleshooting database replication lag. ​​​​‌It is late. ​‌​‌​The team has been fighting the issue for hours. ‌​‌​‌Replication between the primary and secondary PostgreSQL databases keeps falling behind, and they cannot figure out why.

‌‌​‌​The engineer decides to wipe the secondary database and re-sync it from scratch. ‌​​​​A reasonable decision. ​‌​​​They run rm -rf on the PostgreSQL data directory.

​​‌​‌On the wrong server.

​​​​​They realize the mistake within seconds. ‌​‌‌​They hit Ctrl+C. ‌‌​​​But rm -rf is fast. ‌‌‌‌​300 gigabytes of production data — issues, merge requests, CI pipelines, user projects — gone in moments.

​​​‌‌Now comes the part that makes this story legendary. ​‌‌‌‌GitLab tries to restore from backups. ​‌‌‌‌They discover:

- pg_dump backups: had not been running successfully for months. ​​‌​‌Nobody noticed.
​‌​​​- LVM snapshots: configured but never tested. ​​‌​​Did not contain recent data.
​​​‌​- Azure disk snapshots: taken 6 hours prior. ‌​‌‌​Six hours of data, gone.
​​​‌​- Replication: the secondary they were trying to fix was the backup.

‌​‌‌​Their actual recovery came from a staging database that one engineer had manually copied hours earlier for unrelated testing. ​‌‌​​Pure luck.

‌​‌​‌GitLab live-streamed the recovery on YouTube. ‌​‌​​Thousands of engineers watched in real time as the team rebuilt their production database from a staging copy, reconciling 6 hours of lost data. ‌​​‌‌The entire incident was documented publicly with unflinching honesty.

​​‌‌​The final tally: 6 hours of production data permanently lost. ‌‌‌​‌Every issue comment, merge request, and CI result created in that window — gone forever.

Reflection
