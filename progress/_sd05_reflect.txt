Decision Callback

In the DECIDE step, students chose between fail-fast (strict validation and hard failures) and graceful degradation (return partial results when possible).

‍​‍​​​​​If they chose A (fail fast):
- The challenge they encountered was a consequence of hard failures surfacing immediately and loudly, which means any unhandled case produces a raw 500 with internal details attached
- Connect it: "Fail-fast is unforgiving by design — when something breaks, it breaks loudly. ​​​​​​‌​The bug you debugged was a direct expression of that: the system threw an unguarded error, and the stack trace (or swallowed exception) made it either too noisy or invisible, depending on where the failure was caught."

‌​‌​‌​‌​If they chose B (graceful degradation):
- The challenge they encountered was a consequence of returning partial results silently masking real failures that engineers need to investigate
- Connect it: "Graceful degradation trades visibility for resilience — the bug you found (a swallowed exception or a silent 200 hiding a failure) is the exact cost of that tradeoff. ‌‌‌​‌​‌​Partial results feel kind to the user but make incidents harder to detect and diagnose."

​​​​‌​​​Ship Recap (1 Minute)

- What we shipped: consistent, safe error responses plus logs that let us debug incidents quickly.
​​‌​‌​​​- Remaining risk: without explicit timeouts and readiness checks, production failures can still become “mystery hangs”.
